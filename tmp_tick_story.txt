Real-World Case Study: Hershey the Lab Mix — Switching from Simparica to The Resistance by Wildly Blended

Owner: Kyle, Massachusetts (top 10 state for tick population)
Dog: Hershey, 75lb Labrador mix

THE STORY:
Kyle stopped giving Hershey Simparica after finding a paralyzed tick on his bed from biting Hershey. He reasoned: if Simparica paralyzes and kills ticks by affecting their nervous system, what is it doing to Hershey's liver long-term? The drug works systemically — it enters the dog's bloodstream and kills insects that bite. That mechanism raised serious concerns about cumulative liver toxicity.

Living in Massachusetts — one of the top 10 states for tick population — stopping Simparica was terrifying. But Kyle took a leap of faith and switched to a natural protocol.

THE NATURAL PROTOCOL:
Primary product: The Resistance by Wildly Blended — a blend of organic compounds designed to make the dog's blood unattractive and repellent to ticks and fleas. Works from the inside out. The dog's blood chemistry changes so ticks don't want to feed.

THE TEST:
Hershey went hiking in the woods and ran through tall grass for an extended period. 5 hours later, Kyle found two ticks — different species — crawling on his bed. Both ticks appeared completely normal: not swollen, not engorged, no signs of having fed. They had been on Hershey for hours in prime tick habitat in Massachusetts and chose not to bite.

THE SIGNIFICANCE:
- Two different tick species
- Hours of exposure in high-risk tick environment
- Massachusetts has very high Lyme disease risk
- Neither tick fed despite opportunity
- Ticks appeared healthy (not paralyzed or dying like with Simparica)
- This suggests The Resistance works by making blood unattractive, not by killing after the bite

COMPARISON TO SIMPARICA:
Simparica (sarolaner) is a systemic isoxazoline drug. It works by killing ticks AFTER they bite and absorb blood. The tick must bite to be affected. This raises concerns:
1. The tick still bites and can transmit disease before dying
2. The drug circulates in the dog's blood permanently while on the medication
3. Isoxazolines have a black box warning for neurological effects in dogs
4. Long-term liver impact from chronic systemic chemical exposure is a legitimate concern
5. A paralyzed tick found on the bed means the chemical is strong enough to paralyze insects — what is chronic exposure doing to liver enzymes?

THE RESISTANCE approach is fundamentally different — it aims to prevent the bite entirely rather than killing after the bite.

MULTI-LAYER TICK PREVENTION PROTOCOL (what Kyle uses):
1. Internal: The Resistance by Wildly Blended (daily supplement)
2. Topical spray: Natural essential oil-based tick repellent spray before outdoor activity
3. Collar: Natural herbal tick-repelling collar
4. Post-hike: Full tick check after every outdoor session

NATURAL FLEA AND TICK PHILOSOPHY:
Chemical flea/tick preventatives (Simparica, NexGard, Bravecto, Frontline) are systemic neurotoxins or topical pesticides. For dogs with lipomas, liver issues, or inflammatory conditions, reducing toxic load is critical. The liver processes all these chemicals. A dog already dealing with chronic inflammation doesn't need additional chemical burden on top of a poor diet.

The natural approach works best when combined with:
- High quality anti-inflammatory diet (raw, freeze-dried, gently cooked)
- Omega-3 supplementation to support healthy skin (less attractive to parasites)
- Regular grooming and tick checks
- Avoiding peak tick times (dawn/dusk) when possible
