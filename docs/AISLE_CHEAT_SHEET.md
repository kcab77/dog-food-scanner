# 🛒 The Aisle Cheat Sheet

**What to look for, standing in the store, without reading a paragraph.**

This is the reference behind PawGrade's scoring — food on the left of your brain, treats on the
right. They are judged differently on purpose: **food is the entire diet, a treat is a few pieces
a day.** The same ingredient doesn't deserve the same weight in both, and the scorer reflects that
(treat penalties run at 70% of food penalties — except toxic, which never softens).

---

## 🥣 FOOD — what to look for

### The first five ingredients are the food

Everything after is seasoning. Everything after **salt** is essentially dust — salt is required to
be listed in descending order like everything else, so anything below it is present in smaller
amounts than the salt.

| ✅ Want to see | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| A **named whole meat** first — "deboned chicken", not "chicken meal" | "Meat meal", "poultry by-product", "animal fat" — unnamed means unsourced |
| A **named meal** second is fine — "chicken meal" is concentrated protein | Corn, wheat, or soy in the top 3 |
| **Organ meat** anywhere — liver, heart, kidney, spleen | Peas, lentils, chickpeas, potato **stacked** in the top 5 (DCM signal) |
| **Whole produce** you'd recognise — blueberries, kale, pumpkin | "Natural flavour" high on the list — it's covering for something |
| Fat preserved with **mixed tocopherols** | Fat preserved with **BHA, BHT or ethoxyquin** |

### The mineral pack — the tell nobody reads

Turn the bag over and find the vitamin/mineral block. This is where a manufacturer spends money
nobody markets, so it's the most honest signal on the label.

**The ladder: chelate > sulfate > oxide**

| Grade | Looks like | Means |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Best** | zinc **proteinate**, copper **amino acid chelate**, iron **polysaccharide complex** | Bound to protein. Absorbed like food. |
| ⚠️ **Acceptable** | zinc **sulfate**, ferrous **sulfate**, copper **sulfate** | Inorganic, mildly pro-oxidant, genuinely absorbed. Not a reason to reject a bag. |
| ❌ **Worst** | zinc **oxide**, copper **oxide**, manganous **oxide** | Barely absorbed and cheapest to buy. AAFCO won't even let copper oxide count toward a minimum. |

**Always avoid:** menadione (synthetic K3), sodium selenite, sodium selenate.

**⚠️ The copper exception.** Copper runs backwards. It accumulates in the liver and dogs have no
good way to clear it — and **AAFCO deleted the copper maximum in 2007 and never replaced it**, so
"meets AAFCO" tells you nothing about the ceiling. Labradors are a documented copper-storage
breed, along with Bedlington Terriers, West Highland Whites, Dobermans and Dalmatians. For those
dogs, total copper load matters more than the form. Liver enzymes are not sensitive early.

### Format, ranked

homemade > raw > freeze-dried > gently cooked > air-dried > wet > kibble

Kibble isn't punished — it's the neutral baseline. Gentler formats **earn** a bonus.

## 🦴 TREATS — a different set of rules

**The short list is the whole game.** Most good treats have one to three ingredients. A long list
on a treat isn't complexity, it's filler and binder.

### The five-second test

1. **Can you read the whole list out loud in one breath?** That's most of the answer.
2. **Is ingredient #1 an animal?** Beef, chicken, liver, fish — good. Flour, starch, syrup,
   glycerin — the treat is mostly bulk.
3. **Where is the sugar?** Sugar at #1 means the treat is mostly sugar. Sugar at #9 is a trace.
   Position is the information.

### ✅ The best treats in any store

| Treat | Why |
|---|---|
| **Freeze-dried liver / heart** | One ingredient, and the most nutrient-dense thing in the aisle |
| **Chicken feet** | 🦷 Natural chew, scrapes teeth, and the cartilage is joint support |
| **Bully sticks** | Single ingredient, long-lasting, fully digestible |
| **Dehydrated sweet potato** | One ingredient, no protein but nothing to object to |
| **Raw carrot, celery** | 🦷 Free, crunchy, mechanical cleaning, basically zero calories |
| **Sardines** | Omega-3 that actually moves the needle |

### 🦷 For teeth specifically

Mechanical scraping is what works. **Chicken feet · raw carrot · celery · bully sticks · raw
recreational bones.** A "dental treat" made of wheat flour and glycerin is a biscuit with a
marketing claim — chewing shape does some work, but the ingredients do none.

### ❌ What sinks a treat

- **Flour, starch, glycerin or syrup as ingredient #1** — bulk, not nutrition
- **Sugar, corn syrup, molasses high on the list**
- **Artificial dyes** — Red 40, Yellow 5, Blue 2. Colour is for the human buying it.
- **BHA / BHT / ethoxyquin**
- **Propylene glycol** — keeps soft treats chewy
- **Ten-plus ingredients** on something the size of a coin

### ☠️ Never, at any position

**Xylitol** (sometimes labelled *birch sugar*) · grapes · raisins · macadamia · chocolate ·
onion · garlic powder in quantity.

Xylitol is the one that matters most here, because it kills at **treat-sized doses** — precisely
the amount a treat delivers. PawGrade caps any product containing it at 15/100 no matter what
else is in the bag.

## 🥄 Free upgrades already in your kitchen

Worth more than most supplements, and you own them:

**Egg** (whole, cooked) · **sardines** in water · **plain kefir or goat's milk** · **pumpkin**
purée · **blueberries** · **bone broth** · **raw carrot** · **liver**, fresh or freeze-dried

**The 10% rule:** organ meat should be about 10% of the diet, and **liver specifically no more
than about 5%** — it's extraordinarily dense in vitamin A and copper, and more is not better.

---

## The one-line version

> **Food:** named meat first, short list, no corn or wheat up top, chelated minerals, tocopherols
> not BHA.
>
> **Treats:** one ingredient if you can, an animal first, sugar low or absent, and nothing you
> can't pronounce.
