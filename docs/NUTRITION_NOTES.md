## Organs — What's Safe vs The 10% Rule

### True Organs — follow the 10% rule (Vitamin A accumulation risk)
- **Liver** — extremely nutrient dense but HIGH in Vitamin A (fat-soluble, accumulates). Cap at 10% of diet. Best source of Vitamin A, B12, folate, iron, copper.
- **Kidney** — similar caution in very high amounts. Rich in B12, selenium, iron, zinc, riboflavin.
- **Spleen** — fine in moderate amounts but very rich, can cause loose stools if too much.

### Muscle Meats (classified as organs but NO accumulation risk — feed freely)
- **Heart** — muscle meat not a true organ. Rich in TAURINE (critical for heart health), CoQ10, B vitamins, iron, zinc. Can be fed as primary protein. No upper limit.
- **Gizzard** — fine in higher quantities, no accumulation concern.
- **Lung** — lightweight protein, iron. Fine in higher quantities.
- **Tripe** — probiotics, digestive enzymes, balanced omega ratio.

### Key Rule
**Only liver has the strict 10% cap due to Vitamin A.** Heart, gizzard, lung, tripe = free to feed as much as the formula calls for.

## Vitamins & Minerals — Good Forms vs Bad Forms

### AVOID (toxic/accumulate/poor bioavailability)
| Ingredient | Problem | Safe Alternative |
|------------|---------|-----------------|
| Menadione (synthetic K3) | Depletes glutathione, damages liver cells and RBCs, hemolytic anemia | Natural K1 from plants, K2 from animal sources |
| Sodium Selenite | Generates free radicals, progressive kidney tubule damage | Selenomethionine (organic, self-regulated) |
| Copper Sulfate | Accumulates in liver silently over years, chronic hepatitis (Labs, Dobermans especially) | Copper proteinate (organic) |
| Cholecalciferol (synthetic D3) | Narrowest safe range of any dog nutrient, multiple FDA recalls from overdose causing kidney failure/death | Whole food D from fish, egg yolk |
| Zinc Oxide | Poorly absorbed, hemolytic anemia risk, especially Huskies/Malamutes | Zinc proteinate |
| Ferric Oxide | Unabsorbable, used as colorant only, zero nutrition | N/A — just remove it |

### USE WITH CAUTION (poor forms or accumulate over time)
| Ingredient | Problem | Safe Alternative |
|------------|---------|-----------------|
| Retinyl Palmitate / Retinyl Acetate | Synthetic fat-soluble Vitamin A, builds up in liver | Whole food liver (in moderation) |
| Pyridoxine Hydrochloride (B6) | Safe at normal levels, chronic excess causes peripheral neuropathy | Whole food sources |
| Cyanocobalamin (B12) | Inferior form, contains trace cyanide, lower bioavailability | Methylcobalamin |
| Zinc Sulfate | Poor bioavailability | Zinc proteinate |
| dl-Alpha Tocopherol | Synthetic Vitamin E, lower bioavailability than natural form | d-Alpha Tocopherol (natural, note no 'l') |

### PATTERN: Inorganic = bad, Organic/chelated = good
Sulfates, oxides, selenite = inorganic = poor absorption, accumulation risk
Proteinate, chelate, methionine = organic = self-regulated, safer

## Preservatives — Research Backed

| Ingredient | Severity | Key Research |
|------------|---------|-------------|
| BHA | Severe | NTP long-term studies: squamous cell tumors in rats/mice/hamsters. IARC Group 2B possible carcinogen. California Prop 65 listed. |
| BHT | Severe | Causes liver cell hypertrophy, inhibits thyroid peroxidase (disrupts hormone production). Tumor promoter in Food & Chemical Toxicology study. |
| Ethoxyquin | Toxic | Originally a pesticide. FDA requested voluntary reduction 1997. Liver enzyme elevation, kidney lesions, immune dysfunction in dogs. Banned in human food in many countries. |
| TBHQ | Severe | Immune system concerns and tumor development in animal studies at high doses. Banned in several countries. |
| Sodium Nitrite | Severe | Forms nitrosamines during digestion, associated with cancer risk in animal studies. |

## Processing Methods — Ranked Best to Worst

1. **Homemade / Home-cooked** — you control every ingredient, no preservatives, freshest possible. Requires nutritional knowledge to avoid deficiencies long-term.
2. **Raw** — closest to ancestral diet, maximum enzyme and nutrient retention, highest bioavailability. Best option commercially if sourced well.
3. **Freeze-dried** — raw ingredients with water removed at very low temp. Preserves ~95% of nutrients, shelf stable, nearly as good as raw. Great for toppers or travel.
4. **Gently cooked** — low-temp cooking preserves more nutrients than kibble. Still loses some enzymes but whole food ingredients intact. Far better than kibble.
5. **Air-dried** — slow low-heat drying, similar to dehydrated. Better than kibble, not as good as freeze-dried. Check ingredient quality carefully.
6. **Wet / Canned** — whole food ingredients but cooked at high heat (240-250°F), hotter than some kibble. Destroys most enzymes. Better than kibble for hydration and ingredient quality, worse for nutrient retention.
7. **Dry Kibble (extruded)** — worst option. Cooked at 250-300°F under extreme pressure, virtually no enzymes survive. Synthetic vitamins added back post-processing to compensate for what's destroyed. High carb, ultra-processed.

| Method | Temp | Enzyme Survival | Notes |
|--------|------|----------------|-------|
| Homemade | Varies | Varies | Best control, needs nutritional balance |
| Raw / Freeze-dried | None / very low | ~95% | Best bioavailability, most nutrients intact |
| Dehydrated / Air-dried | 104-118°F | Most survive | Right at enzyme threshold |
| Gently Cooked | Low-medium | Partial | Far better than kibble, some enzymes lost |
| Wet/Canned | 240-250°F | Mostly destroyed | Hotter than kibble in some cases, whole ingredients still |
| Extruded Kibble | 250-300°F | Virtually none | Synthetic vitamins added back post-processing |

## Carbs & Labs

- Labs have a documented **POMC gene mutation** making them more carb/glucose driven than most breeds
- Slight energy dip when removing carbs = normal, usually caloric deficit or 2-4 week metabolic adjustment
- Labs do better with ~10-20% carbs in diet vs zero carb
- Best whole food carb sources: cooked sweet potato, white rice, pumpkin puree
- High carbs (>30%) feeds lipoma growth via insulin → fat storage

## Deficiencies to Watch (whole food diet, timeline: months not days)
- **Zinc** — crusty nose/paw pads, dull coat, slow wound healing
- **B vitamins** — low energy, dull coat, neurological signs in severe cases
- **Vitamin D** — muscle weakness (rare with protein rotation)
- **Iron/anemia** — pale gums, lethargy (caught at annual bloodwork)
- **Omega-3** — inflammation flare, lipoma growth (SFP protects against this)
- **Calcium/phosphorus** — only real risk with home-cooked, not commercial
