# 💰 Homemade vs commercial — the real numbers for Hershey

**Question:** would cooking Dr Judy Morgan's recipe be cheaper than the $700+/month currently going to
food and supplements?

**Short answer: yes, roughly half — but the saving is smaller than it looks, and there's a
verified risk that decides how to do it.**

---

## Step 1 — how much food Hershey actually needs

**75 lb = 34 kg.**

```
RER  = 70 × (34)^0.75  = 70 × 14.08  ≈ 986 kcal/day
MER  = RER × 1.4–1.6 (neutered adult, 8 yrs, moderate activity)
     ≈ 1,380–1,580 kcal/day        →  call it 1,500
```

**By weight**, fresh feeding runs 2–3% of body weight daily for maintenance:

```
75 lb × 2.5% = 1.9 lb/day  →  ≈ 56 lb per month
```

Both methods agree. **Budget for ~56 lbs of finished food a month.**

## Step 3 — the finding that decides HOW you do this

**[Stockman et al. 2013, JAVMA](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/236917448_Evaluation_of_recipes_of_home-prepared_maintenance_diets_for_dogs) — 200 home-prepared dog food
recipes from 34 sources, checked against NRC and AAFCO:**

- **95% had at least one nutrient deficiency**
- **84% had multiple imbalances**
- **Only 5 of 200 met NRC requirements — and all 5 were written by veterinarians**
- Only 9 met AAFCO
- **Zinc and copper were among the most common deficiencies**

And the finding that matters most in practice: **most recipes were vague about ingredient, quantity,
preparation, or which supplement to use — so two people following the same recipe produce two
nutritionally different diets.**

### What that means here, specifically

**It is not an argument against homemade.** It's an argument against *improvised* homemade.

- **Dr Judy Morgan is a veterinarian** — her recipes fall in the better-performing group, not the 95%.
- **The danger is substitution.** Swap salmon for chicken because salmon was expensive that week, skip
  the seaweed because you ran out, eyeball the eggshell instead of weighing it — and you have left the
  formulated recipe and joined the 95%.
- **Deficiency is slow and invisible.** Zinc and copper problems take months to show and look like skin
  and coat issues first. You would not connect it to the food.

## Step 4 — what I'd actually recommend

**The full switch is not the best risk-adjusted move.** Three options, cheapest risk first:

### Option A — partial swap (best value for the risk)
Keep **AllProvide as the balanced base at ~50–60%**, cook the rest. You capture perhaps
**$150–220/month** of the saving while a formulated, AAFCO-complete food still guarantees the floor on
zinc, copper and the rest. **If one week gets busy, you feed more of the base and nothing breaks.**

### Option B — full homemade, done properly
Follow the recipe **exactly**, weigh the eggshell, don't substitute. Add a **balancing premix** designed
for homemade diets. Captures the full **$280–410/month**. Costs ~2–3 hours per batch, and batches are
every 1–2 weeks.

### Option C — full homemade, improvised
**Don't.** This is the 95%.

---

## Step 5 — the number this analysis doesn't include

**Your time.** Batching 56 lbs of food is roughly 2–3 hours every two weeks — call it **5 hours a
month.** At Uber Eats rates that's real money, and it's the reason people quit homemade in month three
and go back to kibble, which is worse than where they started.

**Option A survives a bad week. Option B doesn't.** That's the actual argument for A.

---

## One conflict to resolve first

**The heart recipe contains green-lipped mussel. Your lipoma research lists GLM as damp-building and
says avoid it.** Hershey has lipomas, not heart disease — so if you're using this recipe as a template,
**that ingredient comes out.** Decide that before you cook, not after.
