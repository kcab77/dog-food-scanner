# 🪜 The Ladder — what a beginner learns, in order

**The problem this fixes:** the app currently teaches everything at once. A new owner doesn't need
16 sections. **They need one lesson per scan, and by scan five they can read a bag without us.**

**The goal is not "stop feeding kibble."** It's *"buy the best bag you can afford this week."*
Most people upgrade within kibble before they ever leave it, and shaming them out of the category
loses them entirely.

---

## Level 1 — Is a named meat first?

**Look at:** ingredient #1.

**✅ Good:** a named animal — `Chicken`, `Chicken meal`, `Lamb`, `Salmon`, `Beef`.

**❌ Red flag:** a grain, legume or starch at #1 — `Corn`, `Wheat`, `Ground rice`, `Pea protein`.
That bag is mostly not meat, whatever the front of it says.

**That's the whole lesson.** Meat first, or not. It's uncontested, it takes two seconds, and it
separates most of the shelf.

---

### Where whole meat vs meal sits (PawGrade's position: whole meat is better)

**This does not belong in Level 1** — it's a real debate and beginners shouldn't be handed one side of
it as fact. For the record, and for later levels:

**The case for whole meat — and PawGrade's position.** Fresh named meat is less processed. Rendering
runs at high heat, and the source material behind a meal is less transparent than a named muscle meat.
**This is the majority holistic position and it's what the app teaches.**

**The one narrow thing that's also true:** ingredients are listed by weight *before* cooking, and whole
meat is roughly 70% water. **That doesn't make meal better** — what it means is that `Chicken, corn,
wheat, corn gluten meal…` can be a **marketing arrangement**, where the chicken drops several places
after the water bakes off and the bag is mostly grain. **The lesson there is about ingredient
splitting, not about preferring meal.**

**Practical version:** whole named meat first is the goal. **A named meal (`chicken meal`) is
acceptable; a generic meal (`meat meal`, `poultry meal`) is not** — which is Level 2, and is the part
that actually matters most.

## Level 2 — Is the animal named?

**Look at:** every animal word on the label.

| ✅ Named | ❌ Generic |
|---|---|
| Chicken meal · Lamb meal · Salmon meal | **Poultry meal · Meat meal · Meat and bone meal** |
| Chicken fat | **Animal fat** |
| — | **Animal digest** |

**Why it matters, precisely:** *generic means the species isn't disclosed*, which means it can change
batch to batch depending on what's cheap that month. **You cannot get consistency from an ingredient
that isn't named.** That's the argument — not that it's automatically toxic.

**This is the single easiest upgrade to teach.** One word, and anyone can check it.

---

## Level 3 — What's the fat, and what's keeping it fresh?

**Look at:** the fat ingredient and the words right after it.

- ✅ `Chicken fat preserved with **mixed tocopherols**` — vitamin E, natural
- ❌ `Animal fat preserved with **BHA/BHT**` — synthetic, and the species is hidden too

**Why:** fat goes rancid, so it has to be preserved somehow. **Which preservative they chose is a pure
cost decision** — and it tells you what they did on every other line you can't see.

---

## Level 4 — How much of the top 5 is filler?

**Look at:** ingredients 1–5. Count the grains, legumes and starches.

**Accurate framing:** dogs have **no dietary carbohydrate requirement** — but carbs aren't poison. **The
issue is displacement.** Every filler slot in the top 5 is a slot not holding meat. **Three of the top
five being corn, wheat and pea protein means the bag is mostly not meat**, whatever the front says.

**Don't say "grain-free is better."** Grain-free bags routinely replace grain with peas and lentils,
which is the pattern the DCM conversation is actually about. **Fewer fillers beats swapped fillers.**

---

## Level 5 — The mineral block

**Look at:** the long list near the bottom. **Count the word "oxide."**

- ✅ `proteinate` · `amino acid chelate` · `methionine` · `selenium yeast`
- ⚠️ `sulfate`
- ❌ `oxide` — **AAFCO won't even let copper oxide count toward a food's copper minimum**, citing *"very
  poor apparent digestibility"*

**Why this is the best tell on the whole label:** mineral forms are **invisible to marketing**. No bag
advertises proteinates on the front. **Two or more oxides means they bought the cheapest forms
available — and that's what they did everywhere you can't see.**

## What this means for the app

**One lesson per scan, not sixteen sections.**

| Scan | What we teach | What we leave alone |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | Level 1 only — "here's your #1 ingredient and why it matters" | everything else |
| 2nd | Level 2 — named vs generic | |
| 3rd | Level 3 — fat and preservative | |
| 4th | Level 4 — fillers in the top 5 | |
| 5th | Level 5 — the mineral block | |
| 6th+ | Full detail unlocked — **they can now read a bag without us** | |

**The score always shows.** What changes is how much explanation comes with it.

**By scan five they don't need the app to judge a bag — and that's the point.** A tool that makes you
capable is one you recommend to people. A tool that keeps you dependent is one you delete.

---

## The tone that has to survive

> **"I fed my dog kibble for six years. I'm not judging you."**

**Never "this food is bad."** Always *"here's what's in it, and here's a better bag at the same price."*

The person scanning a $22 bag of Dog Chow is doing their best with what they have. **Contempt loses
them — and they're exactly who this was built for.**
