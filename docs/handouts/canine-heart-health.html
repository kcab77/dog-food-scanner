Holistic Canine Heart Health

What to watch for, what to feed, and what to count at home — used alongside your vet's treatment, never instead of it.

Read this first

Everything here runs alongside Vetmedin and prescribed diuretics like Lasix (furosemide). In congestive failure, furosemide is what keeps fluid out of the lungs. Never reduce or replace a prescribed diuretic with an herbal one. Add, tell your vet, and let them adjust.

<30–35 Resting breaths per minute. Count for one full minute while your dog is asleep. A rate climbing over several days means fluid is building — often before any cough. Write it down daily and bring the trend to your vet.

Warning signs

  • The night cough. Coughing at night, or being unable to settle and get comfortable lying down. Lying flat makes it harder to breathe around fluid — a dog that suddenly wants to sleep sitting up is telling you something.
  • Restlessness after dark. Repositioning, pacing, unable to sleep.
  • Rising resting breath rate — the earliest signal of all.
  • Exercise intolerance — tiring on the walk they used to finish easily.

Sudden laboured breathing is an emergency. Go now — don't manage it at home.

Know your breed's risk

Small breeds
Poodles · Cavalier King Charles Spaniels
Mitral valve disease. Usually announces itself as a murmur first, then progresses slowly.
Large breeds
Dobermans · Great Danes
DCM. The more dangerous pattern — it can stay silent, then present suddenly. Screen before symptoms.

What to feed

  • Heart and organ meat — beef heart, pork heart, liver, tongue, gizzards. Like feeds like.
  • Dark meat, not breast. Thighs and legs carry far more of the heart-relevant amino acids — and cost less.
  • Fresh omega-3 — sardines in water, salmon, cod skin, minnows. Lowers inflammation in the heart muscle itself.
  • Raw goat's milk — naturally carries carnitine, taurine, vitamin D and GABA.

TCVM food categories

Single-ingredient products beat blends — blends rarely reach a therapeutic dose of anything.

The mouth–heart connection

Bacteria from dental disease enter the bloodstream and filter directly through the heart valves and kidneys, accelerating damage to both. The consequence catches people out: dental cleanings should still be performed on heart patients — with close anaesthetic and cardiac monitoring — rather than avoided out of fear. Skipping dental care in a heart patient is not the safe choice it feels like.

Where this comes from. The diet and supplement protocols are holistic and TCVM veterinary clinical practice — they are what integrative vets use and the doses they use. They have not been tested against canine heart disease in controlled trials. Resting respiratory rate monitoring and the dental–heart link are the exceptions: both are also standard cardiology practice.

On sudden death. Cardiac disease is among the leading identified causes of sudden death in dogs — but rankings are unstable, and 37% of sudden deaths have no cause found even after necropsy. That uncertainty is the argument for watching for signs, not for panic.
